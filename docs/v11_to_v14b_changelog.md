# What changed from v11 → v14b, and why

*Prepared 2026-06-18 for the project review. This document explains every modelling
change between v11 and the current production-grade model (v14b), the reasoning behind
each one, and the measured impact. All numbers come from the committed result JSONs in
`results/`; the figures in `docs/figures/` are regenerated from those same files by
`scripts/make_v14b_figures.py`.*

---

## 0. The one-sentence story

> We were trying to catch emails that **falsely claim to be from a profiled sender** —
> both LLM-written forgeries and human impostors. The hard part was **generalizing to
> LLM generators we never trained on**. v11→v13 attacked it by adding generators and
> plateaued. v14→v14b solved it by attacking the *authorship model itself*: train on
> **many more identities** (44 → 844 authors) **and** keep the synthetic hard-negatives.
> The cross-generator gap that was stuck since v11 is now closed.

**Headline (held-out Claude + Gemini, generators never seen in training):**

| | v11 | v12 | v14b |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gemini-2.5-flash AUC | 0.614 | 0.746 | **0.953** |
| Claude-3.5-haiku AUC | 0.639 | 0.829 | **0.972** |
| Pool AUC (deploy scorer) | 0.679 | 0.854 | **0.975** |
| Forgeries caught @5% false-alarm | 14% | 39% | **88%** |

See **`docs/figures/fig1_cross_generator_auc.png`** and **`fig2_pool_progression.png`**.

## 1. The task and how we measure it

- **Goal:** given a sender we have profiled (a handful of their real emails), flag a new
  message that is *not really from them* — whether it's an **LLM forgery** or a **different
  human** (impostor).
- **Model:** a LUAR-MUD authorship encoder + LoRA. Each sender gets a style centroid; a
  query is scored by its distance to that centroid. Higher score = more authentically that
  person.
- **North-star metric:** **TPR @ 1–5% FPR** on forgeries (catch rate at a small
  false-alarm budget), with a **guardrail**: `FPR_other` ≤ ~0.10 (don't wrongly accept
  *human* impostors).
- **The honesty rule:** we always evaluate on **held-out generators** (Claude + Gemini are
  *never* in training) and on a **wrong-human** axis. A model that looks great on its
  training generator but collapses on unseen ones is not production-grade.
- **Held-out eval pool:** 264 genuine / 327 LLM-forgery / 600 wrong-human examples across
  44 profiled senders (`results/.../heldoutCG_*.json → probe`).

## 2. Version-by-version

### v11 — single-generator baseline (the problem is exposed)
- **What it was:** authorship backbone + LoRA, synthetic hard-negatives generated by a
  **single** generator (Mistral-family).
- **Result:** in-domain looked fine, but on **held-out** generators it was barely above
  chance — **Claude 0.639, Gemini 0.614**, pool AUC 0.679, only **14%** of forgeries caught
  at the 5% budget.
- **Diagnosis (memory: `multigen-generalization-gap`):** the "LLM-ness" signal was a
  **Mistral-specific shortcut**, not generator-invariant. This is the central problem the
  rest of the project chases.

### v12 — multi-generator training (the first real fix)
- **Change & why:** train the synthetics on **two** generators (GPT-4o-mini +
  Llama-3.1-70B) while **holding out Claude + Gemini**. Hypothesis: *diversity in the
  training adversary transfers to unseen adversaries.*
- **Result:** big jump — **Claude 0.829, Gemini 0.746**, pool AUC 0.854, forgeries caught
  39%. **Hypothesis confirmed.** No authorship regression.
- **Remaining gap:** Gemini stuck at ~0.75 (weakest), and only 39% of forgeries caught at a
  deployable threshold.

### v13 — +3rd vendor + more volume (the plateau)
- **Change & why:** add **DeepSeek** as a 3rd generator and increase per-sender volume.
  Hypothesis: more generator diversity keeps lifting held-out performance.
- **Result:** **plateau** — Claude 0.830, Gemini 0.755 (essentially flat vs v12). Pool AUC
  0.846. Adding generators had **stopped paying off.**
- **Key realization:** the bottleneck was **not the generators**. The model only knew **44
  sender identities**; its notion of "this person's style" was too coarse to tell a good
  imitation from the real person, no matter how many generators we showed it. This pivots
  the strategy from *more adversaries* to *a better authorship backbone*.
  (Two cheap drop-in pivots were tested and **both failed** — zero-shot LLM-text detectors
  score ≈ chance on our few-shot imitation emails, and an off-the-shelf style embedding did
  not beat our finetuned encoder. See `docs/v14_validation_results.md`. This is why the fix
  had to be a **retrain**, not a bolt-on.)

### v14 — identity expansion, 44 → 844 authors (a clean split-of-effects)
- **Change & why:** expand training identities from 44 Enron senders to **844** by adding
  the **Blog Authorship Corpus** (800 authors). Force the contrastive objective across far
  more authors × topics so style decorrelates from content. **Dropped synthetics** for this
  run (a sampler limitation — see below).
- **Result — one breakthrough, two regressions:**
  - ✅ **Content-invariance solved.** PAN cross-topic (a *clean* off-domain test, not in
    training) jumped **0.779 → 0.879** — the first real movement on this stuck problem in
    the whole project.
  - ❌ **Imitation-catching collapsed** (Claude 0.829→0.541, Gemini 0.746→0.573) because
    synthetics were removed — proving the synthetics are *load-bearing* for catching
    forgeries.
  - ❌ **Enron in-domain slices dropped** (register/len 0.95→0.71) — 44 Enron senders
    diluted among 800 blog authors.
- **Why this is useful, not a failure:** it cleanly separated the two levers. **Identity
  diversity → content-invariance; synthetics → imitation-catching.** The production model
  needs *both*. See **`docs/figures/fig3_split_of_effects.png`**.

### v14b — the synthesis (production-grade)
- **The enabling fix:** `SyntheticBalancedSampler` (`src/email_fraud/data/samplers.py`).
  Its old `__len__` bottlenecked an epoch on the **44 synthetic pairs** (~11 batches), which
  starved the 800 blog authors — that's why v14 had to drop synthetics. The fix scales the
  epoch to the real-author pool (~105 batches) while still guaranteeing synthetic pairs each
  batch. **Verified no-op for v11/v12/v13** (all-paired configs are byte-identical); it only
  changes behavior when there are many unpaired real authors.
- **Change:** 844 authors **+** synthetic hard-negatives (GPT+Llama), anti-Goodhart monitor
  `pauc/min_other_synthetic_5pct`.
- **Result — beats v12 on EVERY axis at once:**

  | Axis | v12 | v14b |
  |---|---|---|
  | Held-out Gemini AUC | 0.746 | **0.953** |
  | Held-out Claude AUC | 0.829 | **0.972** |
  | Pool AUC (mahalanobis) | 0.854 | **0.975** |
  | Forgeries caught @5% FPR | 0.394 | **0.879** |
  | PAN cross-topic (content-invariance) | 0.779* | **0.857** |
  | Blog held-out (150 unseen authors) | 0.786 | **0.909** |
  | register:cross / len:short | 0.95 / 0.92 | **0.97 / 0.93** |

  *\*PAN was first measured at v13; v12's 0.779 is the v13-checkpoint reading of the same
  pre-identity-expansion recipe.*

- **Why it works:** ~5× more synthetic exposure per epoch **plus** a sharper 844-author
  backbone, so LLM imitations fall **further** from a well-modeled style centroid —
  regardless of which model wrote them. This validates the core thesis: **better authorship
  modeling is what catches imitations.**

## 3. The confusion matrices (what changed in practice)

**`docs/figures/fig4_confusion_v12_vs_v14b.png`** — both at the same 5%-false-alarm
operating point, on the held-out Claude+Gemini eval (deployment `mahalanobis` scorer):

| | v12 | v14b |
|---|---|---|
| Genuine senders correctly allowed | 251 / 264 (95%) | 251 / 264 (95%) |
| **LLM forgeries caught** | 129 / 327 (**39%**) | 287 / 327 (**88%**) |
| LLM forgeries missed | **198 (61%)** | 40 (12%) |
| Wrong-humans caught | 598 / 600 (100%) | 550 / 600 (92%) |

The story in one picture: **v12 let 61% of forgeries through; v14b lets only 12% through**,
at the same false-alarm rate, on generators it never trained on.

## 4. Deployment: scorer choice and the guardrail

**`docs/figures/fig5_guardrail_scorer_choice.png`.** v14b separates synthetics so well that
anchoring the threshold purely on the (now-easy) synthetic pool mis-places it for the
*wrong-human* axis under the linear scorer:

- `baseline_linear_z3`: catches 94% of forgeries **but leaks 30% of human impostors**
  (`FPR_other` = 0.30) — Goodharted, **do not ship**.
- **`mahalanobis` (DEPLOY):** catches **88%** of forgeries and holds the guardrail at
  **`FPR_other` = 0.083 ≤ 0.10**, AUC 0.975.

> Note: `auc_g_other` (genuine-vs-impostor *ranking*) is 0.96 for **both** scorers — the
> linear scorer separates impostors fine; it just sits at a bad threshold. The fix is the
> scorer/operating-point choice, not more training.

**Recommendation:** ship **`runs/v14b/lora/checkpoint_best.pt` with the mahalanobis
scorer**, operating point set on `min(other, synthetic)`, not the synthetic pool alone.

---

## 5. Honest caveats (unchanged from `docs/v14_results.md`)
- **Blog is partly in-domain** now (v14b trained on it). The *clean* off-domain win is
  **PAN cross-topic (0.857)**; blog (0.909) corroborates but isn't independent.
- Held-out generators (Claude, Gemini) transfer from GPT+Llama training, but a **fully novel
  future vendor** should still be spot-checked before trusting it blindly.
- Operating point should be set on the **worst-case** of the synthetic and wrong-human axes.

### Which W&B panels to trust
- ✅ **Held-out eval** (the summary run / this report, sourced from `results/`) — the only
  cross-version-comparable, task-relevant numbers.
- ⚠️ **`tpr_at_fpr/*`, `pauc/*` on the training runs** — in-distribution **validation**
  diagnostics computed on each run's *own* split (negatives from *that run's* generators).
  Useful for watching a single run converge; **not comparable across versions**. This is why
  v11 shows a high `tpr_at_fpr/all_1pct` (0.742) yet catches only 14% of *held-out*
  forgeries. See §0/§7 of this doc and the report's "trap" section.

### W&B note — keep future runs syncing automatically
Training was launched with `WANDB_MODE=offline` (the eval script also forces offline).
To make new training runs upload live, run with `WANDB_MODE=online` (the key is already in
`.env`), or sync after the fact:

```bash

WANDB_MODE=online python scripts/wandb_heldout_report.py   # summary run (tables+bars+figs)
WANDB_MODE=online python scripts/wandb_build_report.py     # the report page
```
